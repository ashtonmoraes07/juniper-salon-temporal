# Lena Interview Plan — Juniper Salon

## Purpose

Learn how Juniper Salon currently handles the business process in scope, where it breaks down, who is affected, and what a successful improvement would accomplish. The interview is deliberately non-technical: Lena should describe the business, not choose architecture, tools, integrations, or implementation details.

## Guardrails

- Ask one clear question at a time.
- Prefer concrete recent examples over hypothetical answers.
- Use Lena's own words and distinguish facts from interpretations.
- Do not suggest a solution before understanding the current process.
- Do not ask Lena to make technical decisions.
- Ask permission before discussing sensitive customer, employee, or financial information; collect categories and ranges rather than unnecessary personal data.
- Confirm key conclusions before ending.

## Turn Budget

The plan uses at most 24 interviewer questions, leaving at least 26 turns within a 50-turn interview limit for Lena's answers, clarification, and a final confirmation. It is also designed for an approximately 15-minute session: if time is short or answers are detailed, skip questions already answered and prioritize sections 1–5 plus the closing confirmation.

## Opening Script

“Thanks, Lena. I’d like to understand how this work happens at Juniper Salon today, where it becomes difficult, and what a good outcome would look like. I’ll focus on your business needs and real examples. You do not need to make any technical decisions.”

## Interview Questions

### 1. Context and desired outcome

1. What part of the salon’s work would you most like to improve, and why is it important now?
2. If this were working well six months from now, what would be noticeably different for you, your team, and clients?

### 2. Current process

3. Please walk me through the process from the moment it starts to the moment it is fully resolved.
4. Who takes part at each step, and what is each person responsible for?
5. What information is needed at each step, and where does it come from today?
6. What tools, documents, messages, or informal workarounds are used along the way?
7. About how often does this process happen, and when are the busiest periods?

### 3. Users and affected groups

8. Which employees use or depend on this process most often?
9. How do clients experience the process, including any waiting, follow-up, or uncertainty?
10. Are there different types of clients, services, staff roles, or locations that need different handling?

### 4. Failure cases and response behavior

11. Think of a recent time the process went wrong. What happened from beginning to end?
12. What are the most common mistakes, delays, missed steps, or misunderstandings?
13. How does the team notice that something has gone wrong?
14. What does the team do next, who becomes involved, and how long does recovery usually take?
15. Which situations need an immediate response, and which can safely wait?
16. Are there cases where the process should pause for a person to review or approve what happens next?

### 5. Information and communication needs

17. What does each person need to know to take the next action confidently?
18. Which updates should be sent to clients or staff, by whom, and at what moments?
19. What information must be accurate, private, retained, or easy to look up later?

### 6. Business impact and priorities

20. What does the current process cost in staff time, lost appointments or sales, client frustration, or other consequences?
21. Of the problems we discussed, which two or three matter most, and why?
22. What constraints must any improvement respect—for example, budget, staff capacity, operating hours, policies, or change tolerance?

### 7. Measurable success and close

23. What numbers or observable signals would prove the improvement is working?
24. Let me summarize what I heard: [current process], [top failures], [business impact], [priorities], [constraints], and [success measures]. What did I misunderstand or miss?

## Optional Probes

Use only when needed; do not ask all of them.

- “Can you give me a recent example?”
- “What happens immediately before and after that?”
- “How often does that occur?”
- “Who is affected when it happens?”
- “How do you handle that today?”
- “What would a good response look like from the client’s perspective?”
- “Is that essential for the first useful improvement, or could it come later?”
- “How would you measure that?”

## Interviewer Capture Rules

- Record direct answers in `BUSINESS_NEEDS.md`.
- Mark unanswered items as **Unknown / ask Lena**.
- Label interpretations as **Inference—needs confirmation**.
- Capture exact metrics only when Lena provides them; never invent baselines or targets.
- Translate business statements into technical requirements only after the interview, outside the Business Needs section.
